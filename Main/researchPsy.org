In research psychology, the most common terms revolve around study design, 

data analysis, and theory building 

words like hypothesis, variables, reliability, validity, control group, ANOVA, correlation, 

and ethics are part of everyday vocabulary. 

These terms distinguish research psychologists from clinicians, 

since their focus is on producing knowledge rather than giving advice.

📖 Core Research Psychology Terminology
Foundational Concepts
Hypothesis – A testable prediction about relationships between variables.

Independent Variable (IV) – The factor manipulated in an experiment.

Dependent Variable (DV) – The outcome measured.

Control Group – Participants not exposed to the experimental treatment.

Reliability – Consistency of measurement.

Validity – Accuracy of measurement (does it measure what it claims?).

Research Design & Methods

Between-subjects design – Different groups experience different conditions.

Within-subjects design – Same participants experience all conditions.

Random Assignment – Participants randomly placed into groups to reduce bias.

Longitudinal Study – Observes the same individuals over time.

Cross-sectional Study – Compares different groups at one point in time.

Qualitative Methods – Interviews, case studies, thematic analysis.

Quantitative Methods – Statistical tests, surveys, experiments.

Data & Analysis
Correlation – Relationship between two variables (positive, negative, none).

ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) – Compares means across multiple groups.

p-value – Probability that results occurred by chance.

Effect Size – Strength of a relationship or difference.

Regression Analysis – Predicting outcomes based on variables.

Ethics & Communication
Informed Consent – Participants must agree with full knowledge of risks.

APA Style – Standard writing format for psychology papers.

Belmont Report – Ethical principles guiding human research.

Peer Review – Evaluation of research by other experts before publication.
